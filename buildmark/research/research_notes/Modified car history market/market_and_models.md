# Market Size and Business Models: Vehicle History Record for Modified Cars (US, as of Oct 2026)

## 1. US specialty-equipment (aftermarket accessory/performance) spend and modified-vehicle population (SEMA)

### Takeaway
SEMA's 2026 Market Report puts US specialty-equipment sales at $52.92B for 2025 (about 1% growth, flat in real terms), and about one in four vehicle owners bought accessory or performance products in 2025. Segment detail is only public for 2023: accessories/appearance $27.4B, engine/performance $12.6B, wheels/tires/handling $12.4B. SEMA's current enthusiast/vehicle counts are behind the paywall.

### Cited Findings
- 2025: $52.92B in US accessory and performance parts sales. The market is described as "steady and resilient." — [SEMA eNews, 2026 Market Report](https://www.sema.org/news-media/enews/2026/28/heres-what-you-need-know-semas-2026-market-report)
- 2025: about 1 in 4 vehicle owners bought accessory/performance products to personalize their vehicles. More than half of buyers are under 40, and younger buyers lean toward performance and complex builds. — [SEMA eNews](https://www.sema.org/news-media/enews/2026/28/heres-what-you-need-know-semas-2026-market-report)
- 2025: pickups account for 30% of sales, with more than $15B spent modifying pickups. — [SEMA eNews](https://www.sema.org/news-media/enews/2026/28/heres-what-you-need-know-semas-2026-market-report)
- 2024 figure of $52.65B, and 2025 accessories/appearance segment of $27.64B. Truck/off-road suspension $3.15B; off-road/oversize tires $2.23B. 56% of part purchases start online. 2026 forecast $53.69B. All of these come via a third-party vendor summary (Web Shop Manager), not SEMA directly. Treat as secondary. — [Web Shop Manager](https://webshopmanager.com/sema-automotive-ecommerce-outlook/)
- 2023 (SEMA 2024 report): total $52.3B. Appearance/accessory $27.41B; engine/performance $12.56B; wheels, tires and handling $12.38B. The last splits into $5.68B suspension/brake/steering and $6.70B performance, special-purpose and off-road tires plus custom wheels. 58% of accessorized vehicles are used for commuting, so they are mostly daily drivers. Alt-power vehicle owners spent $2.5B on accessories. — [The Shop mag, SEMA 2024 Market Report](https://theshopmag.com/news/sema-releases-2024-market-report/)
- Restyling segment: the latest public stand-alone figure is old, at $5.22B (2016 report). — [SEMA News search result](https://www.sema.org/news-media/magazine/2015/27/restyling-market-showing-healthy-uptick) (dated; do not use as current)
- The full 2026 Market Report is sold at sema.org/research. — [SEMA eNews](https://www.sema.org/news-media/enews/2026/28/heres-what-you-need-know-semas-2026-market-report)

### Inferences
- Roughly 290M+ registered vehicles × "1 in 4 owners" suggests tens of millions of owners buy parts each year. That is the broad addressable base, but most buy light accessories (floor mats, racks). The relevant "modified car" core is closer to the engine/performance (~$12.6B) plus wheels/tires/suspension (~$12.4B) buyers. The vehicle-registration figure here is not sourced in these notes; verify it.
- The pickup and off-road segment ($15B+, 30%) is the largest single niche for modification records. Lift kits, oversize tires and suspension materially affect resale value and insurability.
- About $53B a year of parts installed with essentially no structured record is the core "undocumented value" argument.

### Gaps
- SEMA's current segment split (performance vs. wheels/tires vs. accessories vs. restyling) for 2025 is paywalled. The 2023 split is the latest public one.
- SEMA's count of modified vehicles / "enthusiast" owners in millions was not found publicly. Older SEMA press figures (not verified in this pass) should not be used without a source.

## 2. US used-car transaction volume, private-party share, and enthusiast/collector transaction volume

### Takeaway
About 38–39M used vehicles change hands in the US each year (Cox forecasts 38.3M for 2026, down 0.9%), of which about 20.3–20.4M are dealer retail. That implies private-party plus other non-retail transactions are on the order of 18M, but this is my back-calculation. Online enthusiast auctions are tiny by units but large by value: BaT did $1.713B on 49,486 auctions in 2025. Hagerty insures 2.8M vehicles and did ~$566M in marketplace GMV.

### Cited Findings
- 2026 forecast: total used sales of 38.3M (−0.9% vs. 2025) and retail used sales of 20.3M (−0.7% vs. 2025). Overall used sales ended 2025 up 2.7% vs. 2024. — [NIADA summary of Cox Automotive](https://niada.com/dashboard/used-vehicle-sales-expected-to-dip-in-2026/); [Cox Automotive Dec 2025](https://www.coxautoinc.com/insights-hub/used-retail-vehicle-sales-december-2025/)
- CPO sales were 2.61M in 2025 (+2.1%), and the 2026 forecast is about 2.6M. — [Cox Automotive Dec 2025](https://www.coxautoinc.com/insights-hub/used-retail-vehicle-sales-december-2025/)
- Cox's start-of-2025 forecast was 37.8M total and 20.1M retail. — [NIADA](https://niada.com/dashboard/?p=5454)
- Wholesale auctions (NAAA) sold more than 8M vehicles in 2025 (+3%). — [NIADA](https://niada.com/dashboard/auction-sales-top-8-million/)
- Bring a Trailer 2025: $1.713B in sales (>$1.7B, fourth straight year above $1B). 49,486 auctions, a record, averaging 951 a week and peaking above 1,200. 81% sell-through. 1.65M registered users (+11%, 255K+ new). 48 sales above $1M. 600+ project-vehicle listings. "Restomods" are cited among popular auctions, but there is no count of modified cars. — [The Shop mag, BaT 2025](https://theshopmag.com/news/bring-a-trailer-record-growth-2025/)
- BaT 2024: more than $1.5B in sales. — [The Shop mag](https://theshopmag.com/news/bring-a-trailer-surpasses-1-5-billion-2024-auction-sales/)
- Classic.com: collector auction sales were $2.3B in H1 2025 (+4% vs. H1 2024). Its full-year review says more cars changed hands in 2025 than in any prior year (the page itself returned 403, so this comes from a search snippet). — [Classic.com H1 2025](https://www.classic.com/insights/2025-half-time-report); [Classic.com 2025 Year in Review](https://www.classic.com/insights/2025-year-in-review/)
- Hagerty FY2025: revenue $1,456M (+17%); written premium $1,194M (+14%); 1,684,935 policies in force; 2.8M insured vehicles (+9%); about 930K paid Hagerty Drivers Club members (+6%); membership and other revenue $82M. Marketplace revenue was $119M (+119%), with private sales GMV $286.8M (+271%) and auction sales $278.7M (+56%). 2026 written premium growth guidance is 15–16%. — [Hagerty press release](https://newsroom.hagerty.com/press/hagerty-reports-full-year-2025-results-provides-2026-growth-outlook/); [Hagerty 10-K FY2025](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1840776/000184077626000013/hgty-20251231.htm)
- Cars & Bids: no full-year 2025 total found. The only data point is a newsletter snapshot of about $13M in early-2025 YTD sales with an average sale price of about $27K. This is low quality and partial. — [The Daily Vroom](https://thedailyvroom.beehiiv.com/p/bat-vs-carsandbids)

### Inferences
- Implied private-party plus other non-dealer transactions: 38.3M − 20.3M ≈ 18M in 2026, and similar in 2025. That is a large pool where no dealer supplies a CARFAX and where the seller's own documentation matters most. Cox's definitions of "total" vs. "retail" should be checked before quoting this as "private-party."
- BaT's average sale price is about $1.713B / (49,486 × 0.81 ≈ 40K sold) ≈ $43K. That is high-ticket, documentation-heavy inventory where sellers already upload service records and build sheets, making it the natural wedge market.
- Hagerty's 930K paying members and 2.8M insured vehicles show enthusiasts will pay recurring fees (membership is about $70–90/yr, though the per-member price is not sourced here).

### Gaps
- No authoritative 2025 private-party share from Edmunds or Cox was found.
- Cars & Bids annual GMV is not public in any source found.
- No share of modified vehicles within used or enthusiast transactions was found.

## 3. Vehicle history report market: CARFAX, AutoCheck, pricing, volume, market size

### Takeaway
CARFAX is now disclosed directly. It generated $1,142M of revenue in FY2025 (65% of Mobility Global's $1,750M), growing about 11% a year. It is 81% subscription (dealers) and 85% US, with 40K+ dealer customers and 92K+ service-shop data partners. Consumer report pricing (about $44.99 single; AutoCheck about $29.99) is a minor revenue line. The money is in dealer subscriptions and listings.

### Cited Findings
- Mobility Global (S&P Global Mobility spin-off; Investor Day exhibit dated May 12, 2026). Revenue was $1,485M in 2023, $1,613M in 2024 and $1,750M in 2025 (+8.5%). Q1 2026 revenue was $455M. 2026 guidance is 7.5–9% organic constant-currency growth. — [SEC 8-K Ex. 99.1](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000064040/000110465926058908/tm2613574d2_ex99-1.htm)
- CARFAX revenue was $928M in 2023, $1,039M in 2024 and $1,142M in 2025 (about 11% CAGR). It is 81% subscription and 19% transactional, and 85% US and 15% international. Product mix: Advantage (dealer subscription) 38%, Listings 22%, Financial/Consumer/Other 26%. — [SEC 8-K Ex. 99.1](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000064040/000110465926058908/tm2613574d2_ex99-1.htm)
- CARFAX scale metrics:
  - 38B+ vehicle history records
  - 28M+ VHR views a month (about 336M a year)
  - About 2M "Show me the CARFAX" consumer requests a month
  - 53M+ Car Care users and 23M+ monthly unique visitors
  - 40K+ dealer customers
  - 92K+ service shop partners
  - 1.4M+ annual trade-in leads
  - 1,600+ "Lifetime Dealer" sign-ups since Q4'25
  - Claimed 10–15x dealer ROI
  — [SEC 8-K Ex. 99.1](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000064040/000110465926058908/tm2613574d2_ex99-1.htm)
- The S&P Global board decided in April 2025 to spin off Mobility, with the spin-off expected mid-2026. The renaming to Mobility Global, Inc. comes from an aggregator. — [S&P Global 10-K FY2025](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/64040/000006404026000013/spgi-20251231.htm); [StockAnalysis](https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/mbgl/)
- Consumer pricing (from secondary and competitor sources; conflicting):
  - CARFAX single report about $44.99. Bundles are reported as 3 for $64.99 and 6 for $99.99, or alternatively 3 for $59.99 and 5 for $69.99.
  - AutoCheck single report about $29.99. A 5-pack is about $59.99 per a competitor's reading of the AutoCheck order page on Aug 23, 2026, which includes 21 days of updates. A 25-pack is quoted at $49.99 (suspect).
  - These are all low-quality, competitor-run pages. The CARFAX pricing page (secure.carfax.com) could not be fetched.
  — [CarCheckerVIN](https://www.carcheckervin.com/how-much-is-autocheck); [Collegian sponsored](https://collegian.com/sponsored/2026/05/3-best-carfax-alternatives-in-2026/); [Indie Hackers post](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/discount-carfax-report-why-5-99-gets-you-the-same-data-as-44-99-in-2026-dZ0EtxmUPLX6CSj4NdLq)
- Discount and alternative VHR sellers market reports at about $5.99 (NMVTIS-based). This shows price compression at the consumer end. — [Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/low-cost-carfax-why-5-99-is-the-right-price-for-vehicle-history-in-2026-trKQIVRr7j0Ef4mZ6lTt)
- CARFAX dealer programs: the "Lifetime Dealer" badge launched in January 2022. In July 2025, Lifetime dealers got new-car ad placements on CARFAX Reports, Listings and Car Care through GM's iMR program. — [AutoSuccessOnline](https://www.autosuccessonline.com/carfax-launches-lifetime-dealer-program/); [Seeking Alpha PR](https://seekingalpha.com/pr/20162564-carfax-lifetime-new-car-ads-approved-for-gm-imr-turnkey-program)
- Market-size estimates (LOW QUALITY, paid market-research vendors, methodology unverified):
  - Global VHR market $2.8B in 2025, reaching $4.8B by 2034 (6.8% CAGR). — [Market Intelo](https://marketintelo.com/report/vehicle-history-report-market)
  - "Vehicle data query platform" market $3.47B by 2032 (9.48% CAGR), with VHRs at 52.4% share. — [P&S Market Research](https://psmarketresearch.com/industry-report/vehicles-and-components)

### Inferences
- CARFAX's audited $1.14B (85% US, so about $970M US) is a better floor for the US VHR market than the vendor estimates. The $2.8B global figure is plausible only with Experian AutoCheck and international players added.
- CARFAX already ingests service-shop data from 92K+ shops. Modifications, however, are mostly invisible to it: DIY installs, specialty shops not on CARFAX, and parts and spec detail. This is the white space, but CARFAX is a natural acquirer or competitor if the data proves valuable.
- Consumer one-off pricing ($30–45) sets a willingness-to-pay anchor for a single "build report" purchase by a buyer.

### Gaps
- AutoCheck (Experian) revenue is not disclosed separately.
- CARFAX dealer subscription pricing is not public. No credible source was found.
- Total VHR pulls a year are not disclosed. Only the "28M+ views/month" figure is available.

## 4. Business-model benchmarks (auction fees, shop software, shop lead-gen, data licensing, app subscriptions)

### Takeaway
Enthusiast marketplaces monetize mainly through a 5% buyer's premium (BaT: $250 minimum, $7,500 cap; Cars & Bids reportedly matched this) plus small seller listing fees ($99 to about $429 at BaT). Shops pay about $200–500 a month for management software and about $200–450 a month for lead-gen. Consumer subscription apps convert trials at about 25–46%. Public pricing for insurer or lender data partnerships was not found.

### Cited Findings
- Bring a Trailer:
  - Seller pays $99 for the Classic package and $429 for Plus (professional photography). White Glove is priced by vehicle; one source says $2,500.
  - Buyer pays 5% with a $250 minimum and $7,500 maximum. Older pages cite a $5,000 cap, which looks outdated.
  - These come from secondary comparison sites; bringatrailer.com blocked fetching.
  — [hypercars.io fee comparison 2026](https://hypercars.io/blog/bring-a-trailer-fees); [iSeeCars](https://www.iseecars.com/articles/bring-a-trailer-vs-cars-and-bids)
- Cars & Bids:
  - Historically free for sellers, with a 4.5% buyer fee ($225 minimum, $4,500 cap).
  - The Autopian reports a raise to 5% with a $250 minimum and $7,500 cap, effective Feb 25, following layoffs. The year is not stated in the snippet; the current rate needs confirming. carsandbids.com returned 403.
  — [The Autopian](https://theautopian.com/cars-bids-raises-its-buyer-fees-post-layoffs-to-match-bring-a-trailer); [hypercars.io](https://hypercars.io/auction-fees)
- Tekmetric (2026): Start $199/mo, Grow $349/mo, Scale $439/mo, and custom Enterprise. Annual billing is about $179, $309 and $409. There is no free plan. — [Capterra Tekmetric pricing](https://www.capterra.com/p/190952/Tekmetric/pricing/); [Costbench](https://costbench.com/software/automotive-shop-software/tekmetric/)
- Shopmonkey (2026):
  - Basic $215, Clever $359 and Genius $449 (annual-billing rates per Capterra), with monthly billing about $239, $399 and $499.
  - Extra users cost +$20/mo, and Basic includes 3 users.
  - Conflicting May 2026 snapshot: $179, $292 and $427.
  — [PricingSaaS](https://pricingsaas.com/companies/shopmonkey); [Capterra Tekmetric vs Shopmonkey](https://www.capterra.com/resources/tekmetric-vs-shopmonkey/); [xpay snapshot](https://www.xpay.sh/agent-ready-index/shopmonkey/)
- RepairPal Certified: the fee is not published. Anecdotally it is about $199/mo, plus a possible 10% referral fee on jobs (forum posts; unverified). Yelp agreed to acquire RepairPal for $80M (Nov 2024). — [RepairPal FAQ](https://repairpal.com/faq); [AutoShopOwner forum](https://www.autoshopowner.com/forums/topic/10484-yelp-is-with-worth-it/); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/11/07/yelp-just-spent-80m-on-a-site-for-car-repair-estimates)
- Yelp ads for auto repair: anecdotally about $425–450/mo, with 12-month contracts quoted (shop-owner forum; dated and unverified). — [AutoShopOwner forum](https://www.autoshopowner.com/forums/topic/10484-yelp-is-with-worth-it/); [Tire Review](https://www.tirereview.com/yelp-tire-businesses-what-need-to-know-tire-dealer/)
- Consumer app subscriptions (RevenueCat State of Subscription Apps 2026, covering 115K+ apps and more than $16B in revenue):
  - Median trial-to-paid conversion is 42.5% for trials of 17+ days, vs. 25.5% for trials of 4 days or less.
  - Short-trial conversion is also reported as rising from 42.1% to 46.5%, which conflicts with the 25.5% figure (likely a different metric or cohort).
  - Hard paywalls show 5x better Day-35 trial-to-paid conversion.
  — [RevenueCat blog: free trial length](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/free-trial-length); [RevenueCat benchmarks 2026](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/subscription-app-trends-benchmarks-2026); [RevenueCat report](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps)
- Hagerty Drivers Club: about 930K paid members generating $82M in "membership and other" revenue in 2025. — [Hagerty press release](https://newsroom.hagerty.com/press/hagerty-reports-full-year-2025-results-provides-2026-growth-outlook/)

### Inferences
- A BaT buyer pays up to $7,500 in fees on a ~$43K average car (5% ≈ $2,150). A $30–100 verified build/history report is under 5% of the transaction friction, so it is easy to justify for sellers who want to lift the hammer price.
- Hagerty membership revenue is about $82M / 930K ≈ $88 per member per year (approximate; "other" is included). This is a reasonable ceiling anchor for an enthusiast-consumer annual subscription.
- Shops already pay about $200–500/mo for software and about $200–450/mo for lead-gen. A modification-record product would more likely monetize shops through integrations (Tekmetric/Shopmonkey) or lead-gen ("verified installer" badges) than through a new standalone SaaS line.
- Auction platforms (BaT, Cars & Bids, Hagerty Marketplace) are potential B2B distribution or data partners. They already absorb seller documentation manually.

### Gaps
- No public pricing was found for insurer or lender vehicle-data partnerships (e.g., LexisNexis, Verisk, CARFAX Financial). The CARFAX "Financial/Consumer/Other" line ($~297M, 26% of CARFAX) is the only proxy.
- No car-app-specific subscription conversion benchmark was found.
- RepairPal and Yelp pricing are anecdotal only.

## 5. Comparable provenance/documentation businesses in adjacent markets

### Takeaway
Adjacent provenance models mostly monetize as a cut of a transaction with authentication bundled in (StockX at about 8–9.5% + 3% processing), not as standalone reports. "Carfax for houses" attempts such as Housefax pivoted to dealer-style subscriptions ($89–469/mo for agents, 2015) and never reached scale. Boat, RV and watch analogs were not sourced in this pass.

### Cited Findings
- StockX:
  - Seller fee is about 8–9.5% by seller level (others cite 6–9%), plus a 3% payment processing fee.
  - Authentication cost is bundled into the platform take, for an all-in rate of about 10–12.5%.
  - About $5 minimum fee.
  - Possible failed-authentication penalty of $15 or 15% (lower-quality source).
  — [Small Biz Trends](https://smallbiztrends.com/how-to-sell-on-stockx/); [Think Insights](https://thinkinsights.net/commercial-excellence/stockxs-bid-ask-marketplace-model); [Closo](https://closo.co/blogs/platform-specific-guides/does-stockx-sell-fakes)
- Housefax ("Carfax for houses"):
  - Earlier model: first report free, then $10 per report. It later cut prices amid competition.
  - Oct 2015: launched Housefax Pro unlimited-report subscriptions at $89–469/mo, aimed at real-estate professionals.
  - Reports covered water/fire damage, hazards, meth-lab, CO, and loan/lien history.
  - No current 2026 pricing or status found.
  — [Inman, Oct 2015](https://www.inman.com/2015/10/30/housefax-launches-subscription-service-for-home-history-reports/); [Inman, May 2015](https://inman.com/2015/05/05/housefax-slashes-price-of-property-reports-amid-competition); [Subscription Insider](https://www.subscriptioninsider.com/article-type/news/housefax-launches-housefax-pro-a-subscription-service-for-home-history-reports)
- TrueFax: an app marketed as "a CARFAX for a house," focused on storm damage. No pricing was found. — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id1590607014)
- CARFAX itself monetizes the "lifetime" ownership record through free consumer Car Care (53M+ users), funded by dealer and service-shop marketing. This model, where owners keep records for free and the business side pays, is directly analogous. — [SEC 8-K Ex. 99.1](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000064040/000110465926058908/tm2613574d2_ex99-1.htm)

### Inferences
- The pattern across comparables:
  - Consumer-paid standalone reports struggle (Housefax; VHR price compression to about $5.99).
  - The durable money comes from B2B subscriptions (CARFAX dealers at 81% subscription) or from a transaction take where verification raises the sale price (StockX, BaT).
  - For modified cars, this points to free owner-side logging plus monetizing at sale (seller "verified build" report, or a marketplace or insurer partnership), rather than charging owners a subscription for record-keeping alone.

### Gaps
- Boat/RV history reports (e.g., HullHistory, NMVTIS-based boat checks), watch provenance (Chrono24 Certified, Watchbox, Swiss brand digital passports) and Entrupy authentication pricing were not researched successfully. Searches returned nothing usable within the tool budget.
