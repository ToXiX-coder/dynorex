# Risks for a Modified-Car History Product (legal, regulatory, trust, competitive)

Scope: US, researched 2026-10-16. Not legal advice; items marked [LAWYER] need counsel. "Cited Findings" are sourced; "Inferences" are my reasoning, not verified fact. Many search results were secondary summaries (law firm blogs, trade press); primary documents were fetched where noted.

## 1. Emissions tampering law: exposure for owners, shops, and the platform

### Takeaway
Federal enforcement has softened sharply in 2025-2026. DOJ dropped criminal tampering prosecutions (Jan 2026), and a June 2026 presidential "Freedom to Fix" memo pushes EPA to go easier on good-faith individual repairers. But the Clean Air Act tampering ban, civil penalties, the OBD carve-out, and state law (especially CARB, which fined COBB $1M in Aug 2026) are all still in force. A database of dated, VIN-linked records of non-compliant mods, some confirmed by shops, is discoverable evidence. The biggest exposure falls on the confirming shops and on sellers in CARB and inspection states, more than on the platform. The eBay ruling suggests a platform hosting third-party content has a strong Section 230 defense against civil claims.

### Cited Findings
**Federal policy, 2025-2026**
- EPA's OECA announced a "Compliance First" enforcement posture in December 2025. — [Hunton Nickel Report, Feb 19 2026](https://www.hunton.com/the-nickel-report/doj-curbs-criminal-prosecutions-for-motor-vehicle-tampering-but-noncompliance-remains-a-costly-proposition)
- DOJ stopped pursuing criminal charges for OBD tampering under the CAA and is dismissing existing cases ("more than a dozen prosecutions thus far"), which may affect about 20 or more investigations. DOJ says it will keep pursuing civil tampering enforcement with EPA. — [Hunton](https://www.hunton.com/the-nickel-report/doj-curbs-criminal-prosecutions-for-motor-vehicle-tampering-but-noncompliance-remains-a-costly-proposition)
- Arnold & Porter dates the DOJ order to drop criminal tampering charges to January 2026. DOJ also dismissed U.S. v. Coiteux (OBD) while it was on appeal in the 9th Circuit. President Trump has pardoned some people convicted of OBD tampering. — [Arnold & Porter, July 2026](https://www.arnoldporter.com/en/perspectives/blogs/environmental-edge/2026/07/new-presidential-memorandum-and-epa-guidance-on-vehicle-tampering)
- The June 29, 2026 "Freedom to Fix" Presidential Memorandum directs EPA to clarify tampering policy within 30 days, expedite aftermarket parts testing, consider deprioritizing civil enforcement against individuals acting in good faith, and create alternatives to California's certification process. — [Arnold & Porter](https://www.arnoldporter.com/en/perspectives/blogs/environmental-edge/2026/07/new-presidential-memorandum-and-epa-guidance-on-vehicle-tampering)
- EPA's July 1, 2026 guidance letter says repairs are lawful if the vehicle is returned to its certified configuration before returning to service. It does not explicitly adopt the deprioritization of individuals. A separate EPA letter approves SEMA's aftermarket testing program as a "reasonable basis" under the November 2020 Tampering Policy, with case-by-case review reserved. — [Arnold & Porter](https://www.arnoldporter.com/en/perspectives/blogs/environmental-edge/2026/07/new-presidential-memorandum-and-epa-guidance-on-vehicle-tampering)
- The 2020 Tampering Policy still applies. Conduct affecting an OBD system "may be subject to enforcement regardless of its effect on emissions." The policy focuses on companies and service shops, not individuals fixing their own cars. It treats state anti-tampering laws as applicable and not preempted. — [Arnold & Porter](https://www.arnoldporter.com/en/perspectives/blogs/environmental-edge/2026/07/new-presidential-memorandum-and-epa-guidance-on-vehicle-tampering)
- The CAA has a 5-year statute of limitations, and a future administration could reverse the policy. Many states have their own tampering prohibitions, and some are increasing enforcement. — [Hunton](https://www.hunton.com/the-nickel-report/doj-curbs-criminal-prosecutions-for-motor-vehicle-tampering-but-noncompliance-remains-a-costly-proposition)

**Enforcement history and penalties**
- EPA's defeat-device NECI ran FY2020-FY2023 and produced 172 civil cases with $55.5M in penalties, plus 17 criminal cases with $5.6M in penalties, $1.2M in restitution and 54 months of incarceration. FY2023 included judicial complaints against Diesel Spec Inc. and eBay. EPA's efforts focus on "manufacturing and supply," and it leaves demand-side enforcement (inspections) mostly to states. The page was last updated Dec 15, 2025 and says nothing about renewal. — [EPA NECI page](https://www.epa.gov/enforcement/national-enforcement-and-compliance-initiative-stopping-aftermarket-defeat-devices)
- Premier Performance and affiliates (RallySportDirect, JB Automotive, Stage 3 Motorsports) paid a $3M civil penalty in March 2021 for 64,299 defeat parts sold between 2017 and 2019. — [Manufacturing.net](https://www.manufacturing.net/automotive/video/21319386/premier-performance-takes-3m-hit-over-defeat-device-controversy)
- Sinister Diesel paid $1M in August 2023 ($500K criminal fine plus $500K civil) and is barred from selling delete tuners. — [DOJ EDCA](https://www.justice.gov/usao-edca/pr/california-truck-parts-manufacturer-sinister-diesel-agrees-pay-1-million-after)

**CARB and California**
- In August 2026, COBB Tuning Products settled with CARB for $1,000,000 under the Aftermarket Parts Regulation and agreed not to sell or advertise non-exempted parts in California. — [CARB COBB settlement](https://ww2.arb.ca.gov/cobb-tuning-products-llc-settlement)
- Other CARB aftermarket settlements: Turn 14 Distribution, $1,004,000 (2025); Stillen, $14,000 (2025); Mike's Radiator Muffler Service (an installer/shop). — [CARB Turn 14](https://ww2.arb.ca.gov/node/42501); [CARB Stillen](https://ww2.arb.ca.gov/node/45371); [CARB Mike's Radiator](https://ww2.arb.ca.gov/mikes-radiator-muffler-service-incorporated-settlement)
- In CARB's words, any part that alters an emissions control device must have an Executive Order, and these laws apply to "manufacturers, distributors, dealers, installers, and retailers," even out-of-state ones. Modifying a new vehicle before the title transfers is illegal. CARB has fined dealers for selling modified new vehicles. — [CARB advisory 339](https://ww2.arb.ca.gov/sites/default/files/classic/enf/advs/advs339.pdf); [CARB AutoAnything release](https://content.govdelivery.com/accounts/CARB/bulletins/234f8d2)
- Replacement parts that are functionally identical to OEM need no CARB approval. Modified or add-on parts need approval before they are sold, advertised or installed in California, which takes about 90 days on average per CARB. — [Arnold & Porter](https://www.arnoldporter.com/en/perspectives/blogs/environmental-edge/2026/07/new-presidential-memorandum-and-epa-guidance-on-vehicle-tampering)

**Platform liability analogs**
- In U.S. v. eBay (E.D.N.Y., Sept 2024), the court dismissed the government's civil suit over 343,000 defeat devices sold by third parties. It held that Section 230 applied, that eBay was not the "seller," and that eBay's neutral tools did not materially contribute. The government appealed and reportedly dropped the appeal around April 2025 (not verified on the docket). Section 230 would not have applied to a criminal claim. A similar 2024 ruling, U.S. v. EZ Lynk (S.D.N.Y.), applied Section 230 to a company whose platform carried defeat-device-related software. — [Eric Goldman blog](https://blog.ericgoldman.org/?p=26894); [Sidley](https://environmentalenergybrief.sidley.com/?p=5876); [Value Added Resource](https://www.valueaddedresource.net/ebay-cites-section-230-protections-in-epa-lawsuit-motion-to-dismiss/)

**RPM Act**
- The RPM Act session in which it had more than 165 cosponsors (2021-2022, H.R. 3281/S. 2736) ended without passage. I found no verified 2025-2026 status. — [AftermarketNews](https://www.aftermarketnews.com/?p=220766)

### Inferences
- **Evidence exposure.** A shop-confirmed entry such as "installed DPF delete tune, 2026-03-02" is an admission against the shop, and CARB treats installers as liable. Shop attestation is therefore the feature most likely to drive shops away for emissions-related mods. Owner-entered records could also be subpoenaed or found by state inspectors, insurers, or plaintiffs in a later suit. [LAWYER: subpoena response policy, data retention, and whether to accept records of known-illegal mods at all.]
- **Mitigation options:**
  - Make the parts taxonomy compliance-aware. Capture the CARB EO number (verifiable on CARB's EO database) and show a "CARB-exempt / EPA-reasonable-basis / off-road-only / unknown" status.
  - Refuse, or do not request shop attestation for, categories that are per se illegal (delete pipes, EGR/DPF/cat deletes, OBD-disabling tunes).
  - Let owners record "returned to stock" events. The July 2026 EPA letter makes return-to-certified-configuration the clearest lawful path, which a buyer would value.
  - Keep data minimization and short retention for anything flagged non-compliant.
- **Platform liability.** The eBay and EZ Lynk rulings suggest strong civil protection for hosting third-party records. The platform loses that protection if it authors or labels content in ways that "materially contribute" (for example, recommending tunes or selling parts), and it gets no protection from criminal law. Earning affiliate or parts revenue on defeat parts would undercut the defense and invite CARB "retailer" treatment.
- **Political risk cuts both ways.** Federal leniency (2026) lowers short-term risk. But the 5-year limitations period means records created now could still be used under a stricter future administration. California stays aggressive (COBB, Aug 2026).

### Gaps
- I could not obtain BaT, Cars & Bids, or forum policies on "off-road use only" or non-compliant listings. Searches returned nothing usable. Check their seller terms directly.
- No verified current RPM Act bill number or status for 2025-2026.
- The DOJ memo text is not public (per Hunton). I found no data on state inspection programs, such as which states run visual tampering checks.
- I found no case where a vehicle history or build-log record was used as evidence in a tampering action. This is an unverified risk, not an observed one.

## 2. Liability for inaccurate records, Section 230, and FCRA

### Takeaway
Carfax has been sued repeatedly over incompleteness: a consumer class settlement required a prominent "may not be complete" warning, and a dealer antitrust mass action was mostly lost by the dealers. Its defense combines disclaimers, a limited "Buyback Guarantee," and advice to get an inspection. Disclaimers alone do not always bar claims (Georgia's Raysoni case). For seller-authored records, Section 230 case law (Cohen v. Copart, Hiam v. HomeAway, eBay) suggests strong protection for the platform. The seller remains liable for fraud or misrepresentation. I found no ruling that vehicle history reports are FCRA "consumer reports"; that is unverified either way.

### Cited Findings
- **Consumer class action (2006-2007).** The settlement required Carfax to include a prominent warning in marketing that its reports may not be complete. — [WardsAuto](https://wardsauto.com/dealers/if-the-fax-don-t-fit-then-can-you-acquit-)
- **West v. Carfax.** Plaintiffs alleged Carfax implied its accident data covered all 50 states when police accident data from 23 states was missing. Public Citizen objected to a settlement that gave no damages. — [Public Citizen](https://www.citizen.org/litigation/west-v-carfax-inc-and-polk-carfax-inc/)
- **Raysoni v. Payless Auto Deals (Ga. Sup. Ct.).** The court reversed dismissal of a buyer's fraud claim involving a dealer and a Carfax report. It held that an "as is" clause qualified by surrounding language was not a full disclaimer, so reliance claims could proceed. — [CVN](https://blog.cvn.com/carfax-report-consumer-fraud-trial-to-begin-in-fulton-county-georgia)
- **Dealer antitrust mass action (S.D.N.Y., filed 2013).** About 530 dealers sued. Judge Alison Nathan granted Carfax partial summary judgment, and the action was dismissed. — [F&I Magazine](https://beta.fi-magazine.com/news/mass-action-lawsuit-against-carfax-dismissed)
- **AutoCheck (Experian) v. Carfax.** A court refused to dismiss defamation claims over Carfax's statements about AutoCheck reports. — [Courthouse News](https://www.courthousenews.com/?p=276703)
- **Cohen v. Copart (Cal. Super. Ct., 2022).** Section 230 protected Copart from claims over a seller-supplied "Estimated Retail Value" on vehicle listings. — [Eric Goldman blog](https://blog.ericgoldman.org/?p=24532)
- **Hiam v. HomeAway (D. Mass. 2017).** Section 230 barred claims over a fraudulent listing even where the platform "adopted" it. — [Eric Goldman blog](https://blog.ericgoldman.org/archives/2017/08/section-230-helps-vrbo-defeat-claim-over-fraudulent-listing-hiam-v-homeaway.htm)
- **Limits on Section 230.** Immunity did not cover the platform's own misrepresentations to users (Bride v. YOLO, 9th Cir. 2024) or its role facilitating transactions (Turo v. City of LA). Planet Green v. Amazon (cert petition No. 24-1299) asked whether Section 230 covers platforms that knowingly facilitate sales of misrepresented products; I did not confirm its status. — [EPIC](https://epic.org/ninth-circuit-recognizes-section-230-not-a-get-out-of-jail-free-card-for-tech-companies-that-allegedly-lie-to-users/); [SCOTUS petition](https://www.supremecourt.gov/DocketPDF/24/24-1299/363545/20250618172717345_24-__PetitionForWritOfCertiorari.pdf)
- **FCRA.** I found no court ruling on whether a vehicle history report is a "consumer report." Nearby authority: the D.C. Circuit (2021) held that FMCSA is not a consumer reporting agency when it distributes driver safety records, applying the statute's definitions. — [D.C. Cir. opinion](https://media.cadc.uscourts.gov/opinions/docs/2021/09/19-5321-1915532.pdf)

### Inferences
- **Section 230 design rules.**
  - Keep seller-authored content clearly labeled as such ("Owner-reported," "Shop-confirmed by X on date").
  - Avoid platform-authored badges that read as a guarantee, such as "Verified accurate." Bride, Turo and Planet Green show the risk in platform-made representations.
  - A "verified" badge based on the platform's own checks probably moves those statements outside Section 230.
- **Disclaimer pattern from Carfax's history.** Use a prominent completeness warning, a recommendation to get a pre-purchase inspection, and arbitration/class waiver terms. The Raysoni lesson is that the disclaimer must not be qualified or contradicted by marketing claims.
- **FCRA.** These reports are about the vehicle, not a consumer's eligibility for credit, insurance or employment, so FCRA likely does not apply. But a product that ties a record to a named owner and is used by insurers or lenders could drift toward it. [LAWYER: confirm FCRA non-applicability and the effect of any insurer data-sharing.]
- **Seller liability.** Sellers who fabricate records face state UDAP and fraud claims. The platform should keep audit logs, with edit history visible to buyers, so it can show it did not alter content.

### Gaps
- I could not fetch Carfax's Terms of Use (HTTP 403), so I cannot quote its current disclaimer or arbitration language: [Carfax ToS](https://www.carfax.com/company/terms-of-use).
- No details on AutoCheck's own disclaimers or lawsuits against AutoCheck. Current status of the AutoCheck v. Carfax and West cases is unverified. Carfax's Buyback Guarantee terms are only in secondary sources.

## 3. Trademark: using "Carfax" in positioning

### Takeaway
Carfax actively enforces its mark, including against sound-alikes. It won a TTAB cancellation against AAA's "AAA CarFacts" and has filed UDRP complaints over carfax-like domains. Using "Carfax" in a product name, domain or logo is high-risk. Nominative comparative use in copy ("unlike Carfax...") is generally safer. [LAWYER]

### Cited Findings
- **AAA CarFacts.** The TTAB granted Carfax's petition to cancel AAA's "AAA CarFacts" registration for vehicle history reports, finding a likelihood of confusion with Carfax's marks. Carfax had cited 11 CARFAX-formative marks. — [VitalLaw](https://www.vitallaw.com/news/trademark-ttab-auto-club-s-aaa-carfacts-mark-cancelled-confusion-with-carfax-likely/ipm016363afd87c2a1000a7d690b11c18cbab09)
- **UDRP domain complaints.** Carfax brought complaints against <mycarfax.net>/<mycarfax.org> and against Mark Hannifin. In one case a panel found a name like "CarFacX" would trade on the "identically sounding" mark. — [ADR Forum 1479897](https://www.adrforum.com/DomainDecisions/1479897.htm); [ADR Forum 1469690](https://www.adrforum.com/domaindecisions/1469690.htm)
- **Registrations.** Carfax holds multiple CARFAX registrations, including Reg. 4932234 (vehicle history information and an online marketplace) and Reg. 5518216. — [Justia 86739733](https://trademarks.justia.com/867/39/carfax-86739733.html); [Justia 87702634](https://trademarks.justia.com/877/02/carfax-87702634.html)

### Inferences
- **Avoid "-fax" or "car-facts" sound-alike names and "Carfax for mods" taglines in brand, domain and ads.** The AAA outcome shows that even a descriptive "CarFacts" lost.
- "Carfax for X" pitch language in investor decks is lower risk than public marketing, but the line blurs once it is posted publicly.
- Buying Carfax keywords for ads is a separate question. [LAWYER]

### Gaps
- I found no lawsuit specifically about "Carfax for X" phrasing. I did not search TTABVUE for Carfax oppositions.

## 4. Data trust: making owner-entered records credible, and fraud patterns

### Takeaway
Fraud on vehicle history is common and growing. Carfax estimates more than 2 million vehicles on the road with rolled-back odometers (as of about 2025), and NHTSA's long-cited estimate is about 450,000 vehicles sold per year with false readings. Owner-entered records are inherently weak evidence. Carfax itself keeps owner DIY entries in its Car Care app private, not on public reports.

### Cited Findings
- **Odometer fraud estimates.** NHTSA estimates about 450,000 vehicles sold annually with false odometer readings, costing buyers more than $1B a year. This figure appears to come from an older study. A January 2025 article cites Carfax counting 2.14M vehicles on the road with false odometers, up 18.5% since 2021. Digital odometers make rollback easier. — [NHTSA](https://www.nhtsa.gov/es/seguridad-de-vehiculos/fraude-de-odometro); [Cowboy State Daily, Jan 2025](https://cowboystatedaily.com/2025/01/25/aaron-turpen-odometer-fraud-is-getting-more-commonplace-thanks-to-electronics); [ConsumerAffairs](https://www.consumeraffairs.com/news/even-in-the-digital-age-odometer-fraud-is-a-growing-problem-121620.html)
- **Carfax Car Care.** The app lets owners log DIY or non-network service, but per users those entries do not appear on public Carfax reports. Not all shops report to Carfax. — [BITOG forum](https://bobistheoilguy.com/forums/threads/carfax-car-care-app-user-dashboard.353820/); [Apple App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/carfax-car-care/id552472249)

### Inferences
- **Credibility ladder.** Display the evidence level per entry, from weakest to strongest:
  1. Owner claim
  2. Plus receipt photo (with EXIF/upload timestamp and a document hash)
  3. Plus parts serial number or CARB EO
  4. Plus shop attestation from a verified business account
  5. Plus a cross-check against a third-party record (e.g., Carfax or AutoCheck odometer readings)
- **Fake receipts.** Receipts are easily forged with image and AI tools, so treat them as weak evidence unless the issuer confirms them. Shop-software integrations are stronger.
- **VIN without exposure.** Show a partial VIN publicly (last 6 digits), and give buyers full-VIN match confirmation by asking them to enter the VIN they see on the car.
- **Odometer.** Show the odometer at each entry and flag readings that go backwards. Record entries as append-only with visible edit history.

### Gaps
- No primary 2025-2026 Carfax odometer report. No data on fake-receipt prevalence in vehicle sales.

## 5. Privacy: VINs, location, CCPA/CPRA, DPPA

### Takeaway
VINs are not per se personal information, but under CCPA they are fact-specific and become PI when linked to an owner account, which this product does. The DPPA restricts personal information obtained from state DMV records, not owner-entered data. It matters only if the product pulls DMV data, for example via title-history vendors. Photos and receipts can leak addresses, names and geolocation.

### Cited Findings
- The California AG has said that whether a VIN alone is personal information is a "fact-specific determination." — [Fox Rothschild](https://dataprivacy.foxrothschild.com/2020/06/articles/california-consumer-privacy-act/ccpa-regulations-vin-i-vidi-vici/)
- The DPPA protects DMV-sourced owner name, address, phone and driver ID. Title, odometer, salvage and lien data are typically available in history reports. — [Wikipedia: DPPA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Driver%27s_Privacy_Protection_Act); [FedLaws](https://fedlaws.org/vehicle-owner-lookup-by-vin-or-plate-dppa-rules-and-dmv-requests/)

### Inferences
- **Treat VIN plus account as personal information** and build CCPA/CPRA rights (access, deletion) in from the start.
- **Deletion conflicts with the product's purpose.** If the record transfers with the car, decide who controls it after sale: the previous owner's deletion right versus the buyer's interest. [LAWYER]
- **Strip location data.** Remove EXIF GPS from photos and auto-redact names and addresses on receipts.
- **DPPA.** If title or registration data is sourced via vendors, use DPPA-permissible-use contracts. [LAWYER]

### Gaps
- I did not verify current CPRA regulations on vehicle data, or other state privacy laws (e.g., in Texas, Colorado, Virginia) for VIN treatment.

## 6. Copycat and incumbent risk

### Takeaway
Carfax already has the key building blocks. Its Car Care app accepts owner-logged and non-network service, it has a network of 90,000+ partner shops, and it is adding VIN-specific features (a reliability forecast, April 2026). Adding a "modifications" field would be technically easy. Its incentives are a different matter: it is dealer-focused and wary of liability. I found no evidence that Hagerty, BaT or Cars & Bids are building mod-history products; that remains unverified.

### Cited Findings
- **Partner shops.** Carfax Car Care has 90,000+ partner shops (page possibly stale). — [Carfax Service Shops](https://www.carfaxserviceshops.com/)
- **Owner and non-network logging.** A recent Car Care update improved onboarding for logging home or non-Carfax-shop service. — [Apple App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/carfax-car-care/id552472249)
- **VIN-specific reliability.** In April 2026 Carfax launched a VIN-specific future-reliability feature on its reports. The parent company is referred to as S&P Global Mobility. — [PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/carfax-report-now-delivers-future-reliability-based-on-a-vehicles-unique-vin-specific-history-302735240.html)
- **Data scale.** Carfax claims more than 35 billion records. — [About Carfax](https://www.carfax.com/company/about)

### Inferences
- **Moat.** Carfax probably will not publish owner-entered mod claims, since that conflicts with its accuracy posture and its practice of keeping DIY entries private. The defensible ground is enthusiast-specific trust plus legal/compliance labeling, not raw data.
- **Most plausible fast-follower.** Hagerty, BaT or Cars & Bids adding a "build sheet" to listings, because their sellers already write mod lists in listing text. Shop-management software (e.g., Tekmetric, Shopmonkey) could export install records. [Unverified; needs a dedicated search.]

## 7. Lessons from failed adjacent startups

### Takeaway
DriveTribe, an enthusiast social platform backed by Clarkson, Hammond and May, shut down in January 2022. It depended on ad revenue, which collapsed when the chip shortage cut automaker marketing budgets, and it had already lost $16M in its first two years. The blockchain vehicle-history projects (carVertical, VINchain) I checked were not confirmed dead. carVertical is listed as alive, and the others appear dormant or academic.

### Cited Findings
- **DriveTribe shutdown.** DriveTribe closed in January 2022, citing "significant industry challenges." Automaker ad budgets cut because of the chip shortage hit its core advertising revenue. It reported a $16M loss over 2016-2018. Users could download their content before closure. — [The Drive](https://www.thedrive.com/news/43814/drivetribe-is-finally-shutting-down-for-good); [TTAC](https://www.thetruthaboutcars.com/2022/01/chip-shortage-claims-an-unusual-victim-drivetribe/)
- **carVertical** is listed as alive at seed stage (CB Insights). VW partnered with carVertical. VINchain's status is unclear: it was a 2017 ICO-era project from Estonia. "CarChain" was an academic prototype. — [CB Insights](https://www.cbinsights.com/company/carvertical); [CoinGeek](https://coingeek.com/volkswagen-partners-carvertical-automotive-blockchain-apps/); [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/332351650_CarChain_A_Novel_Public_Blockchain-based_Used_Motor_Vehicle_History_Reporting_System)

### Inferences
- **Avoid ad-only revenue from automotive marketing budgets,** since they are cyclical (the DriveTribe lesson). Monetize the transaction moment instead: a seller-paid report, or a fee from a dealer or auction platform.
- **Blockchain gives tamper-evidence, not truth.** Garbage-in still applies, and owner entries remain unverified. Hash-chained audit logs give the same benefit without crypto baggage.
- **Data portability on shutdown** (as DriveTribe offered) is a trust feature worth promising up front.

### Gaps
- I did not identify specific shut-down build-log or garage apps with documented post-mortems (e.g., the fates of Fuelly, myGarage or Road Trip-style apps), because search time ran out. This needs a dedicated search.
