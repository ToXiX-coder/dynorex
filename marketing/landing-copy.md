## The problem

You put real money into your car. Then a buyer asks what's been done, and all you have is your word, a folder of
receipts and a forum thread from three years ago. Dealers knock money off for "modified." Buyers assume the worst.

## What you get

**The shop backs you up.** Send the shop a one-time link. They confirm the job in two taps, no account needed.
Buyers see "Confirmed by [shop] on [date]."

**Buyers see what backs each mod.** Every entry shows whether it's owner-entered, has paperwork, has a part
number, or was confirmed by the shop. Nothing is dressed up.

**Nothing disappears.** Edits and removals stay on the record, so a buyer knows nothing was cleaned up the week
before the sale.

**The VIN stays private.** A buyer types the VIN on the car and sees match or no match. They never see yours.

**The record goes with the car.** When you sell, give the buyer a code. The whole history lands in their garage.

## How it works

1. Add your car and log your mods. Snap the receipt.
2. Send the shop a link to confirm their work.
3. Share your record with buyers, or paste the build sheet into your listing.
4. Sold it? Hand over the record with a transfer code.

## FAQ

**Is this a vehicle history report?** No. It doesn't cover accidents or titles. It covers what's been done to the
car, and who says so.

**Can't people fake receipts?** They can, which is why a receipt is only one step. Buyers can see which mods the
shop confirmed and every edit made.

**What about emissions?** Engine, exhaust and electronics mods carry an emissions status, with CARB EO numbers
where they exist. Shops are never asked to confirm work that's not street legal.
